\section{Introduction}

Tensor-train policy iteration (TTPI) uses low-rank tensor approximations to make
hybrid control tractable in discretized state-action spaces
\cite{shetty2024generalized,oseledets2011tensor}. Its favorable scaling depends
on the ranks of the tensor-train representation, and those ranks are not an
intrinsic property of the unordered variables. They are the ranks of sequential
unfoldings induced by a chosen mode order. This makes action-mode ordering a
first-class algorithmic variable for TTPI rather than a cosmetic implementation
detail.

The initial physical intuition is simple: action variables that interact through
the dynamics, reward, or value backup should not be separated across many tensor
cuts. However, a physics-only ordering objective is not by itself a TT-rank
objective. Weighted linear arrangement captures the total area under a weighted
cut profile, while peak memory and TT-Cross workload are often governed by the
worst cut. This mismatch motivates a rank-aware extension that keeps hybrid
actuator blocks contiguous and optimizes cut-level bottlenecks rather than only
physical adjacency.

The paper is therefore framed around rank-aware, block-preserving mode
reordering for TTPI. Physics-aware coupling is used as a prior; sensitivity
weights, singular-spectrum estimates, and actual TT rank diagnostics are used to
test whether the prior is aligned with TT complexity. The current
repository-synchronized evidence is tracked conservatively in
\path{notes/claims.md}: pilot HardMove runs support resource-sensitivity
claims, but do not yet support a claim that ordering improves final control
performance.
